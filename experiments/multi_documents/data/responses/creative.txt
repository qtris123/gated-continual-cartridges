14. Q: Create an analogy comparing AMD's fabless manufacturing model and PepsiCo's vertically integrated model to two different approaches to running a restaurant.
    A: AMD is like a celebrity chef who designs brilliant recipes (chip architectures) but doesn't own a kitchen. He rents TSMC's Michelin-starred kitchen for the complex dishes (advanced 7nm and below), uses GLOBALFOUNDRIES' kitchen for simpler fare (larger process nodes), and hires third-party caterers (ATMP partners) for plating and delivery. If any kitchen has a fire or gets overbooked, the chef can't serve. PepsiCo is like a massive restaurant chain that owns its farms (agricultural supply chain), factories (manufacturing plants), delivery trucks (DSD fleet), and many of its restaurants (company-owned bottling). It also franchises to independent bottlers. If one supplier falters, PepsiCo has redundancy. The tradeoff: AMD's model is capital-light and flexible ($450M capex); PepsiCo's is capital-heavy but resilient ($5.2B capex).
