1. Q: Compare the competitive landscapes described in AMD's and PepsiCo's 10-K filings. Who are the primary competitors named by each company?
   A: AMD names Intel Corporation as its primary competitor across Data Center, Client, and Embedded segments, and NVIDIA as its principal competitor in GPUs and a Data Center rival. Additional Embedded competitors include Lattice Semiconductor, Broadcom, Marvell, Analog Devices, Texas Instruments, and NXP. PepsiCo names The Coca-Cola Company as its primary beverage competitor and lists Campbell Soup, Conagra Brands, Hormel Foods, Kellogg, Keurig Dr Pepper, Kraft Heinz, Link Snacks, Mondelēz International, Monster Beverage, Nestlé, Red Bull, and Utz Brands as other competitors.

2. Q: Summarize the key shared risk factors and the key unique risk factors highlighted by AMD and PepsiCo in their respective 10-K filings.
   A: Shared risks include macroeconomic/global uncertainty, cybersecurity threats, foreign currency fluctuations, supply chain disruptions, customer concentration, regulatory and legal risks, and talent retention. AMD uniquely emphasizes Intel's market dominance, semiconductor cyclicality, reliance on third-party foundries (TSMC, GLOBALFOUNDRIES), product security vulnerabilities, and acquisition integration (Xilinx, Pensando). PepsiCo uniquely highlights the Russia-Ukraine conflict's direct impact on operations, water scarcity, consumer health/wellness preferences, brand reputation damage, product recalls/food safety, commodity and input cost inflation, and changing retail landscape dynamics.

3. Q: Compare the revenue recognition policies described in AMD's and PepsiCo's 10-K filings. What are the similarities and differences?
   A: Both recognize revenue upon transfer of control at shipment or delivery, exclude sales/value-added taxes, and offer credit terms of roughly 30-60 days (AMD) and 30-90 days (PepsiCo). Both reduce revenue for sales incentives and promotional programs. AMD distinctly addresses distributor rights of return and price protection with variable consideration estimates, and semi-custom/non-custom product distinctions. PepsiCo distinctly addresses DSD product removal/replacement of damaged or out-of-date goods and concentration of credit risk from major customers like Walmart.

4. Q: How do AMD's and PepsiCo's business models differ with respect to manufacturing and supply chain? Summarize each company's approach.
   A: AMD operates a fabless semiconductor model, outsourcing wafer fabrication to foundries (primarily TSMC for advanced nodes and GLOBALFOUNDRIES for larger nodes) and assembly/test/packaging to third-party ATMP partners. PepsiCo owns and operates manufacturing plants, bottling facilities, warehouses, and distribution centers worldwide, using a mix of direct-store-delivery (DSD), customer warehouse, and distributor networks, and also relies on authorized bottlers and contract manufacturers.

5. Q: List all the geographic regions or countries where AMD and PepsiCo each report having significant revenue or operations, based on their respective 10-K segment disclosures and geographic breakdowns.
   A: AMD breaks down revenue by billing location: United States, China (including Hong Kong), Japan, Europe, Taiwan, Singapore, and other countries, with 66% of revenue from international sales. PepsiCo organizes into seven geographic/functional divisions — FLNA and QFNA (U.S./Canada), PBNA (U.S./Canada), Latin America, Europe, AMESA (Africa, Middle East, South Asia), and APAC (Asia Pacific, Australia, New Zealand, China) — with 43% of revenue from outside the U.S. and the largest international markets being Mexico, Russia, Canada, China, the U.K., and South Africa.

6. Q: Compare the capital allocation strategies of AMD and PepsiCo as described in their 2022 10-K filings, including dividends, share repurchases, and acquisitions.
   A: AMD does not pay dividends and returned $3.7 billion to shareholders entirely via share repurchases in 2022, while making major acquisitions (Xilinx for $48.8 billion and Pensando for approximately $1.9 billion). PepsiCo has paid consecutive quarterly dividends since 1965, paying $6.2 billion in dividends and $1.5 billion in share repurchases in 2022, and divested its Tropicana/Naked juice brands to PAI Partners rather than making major acquisitions.

7. Q: Compare how AMD and PepsiCo each describe their approach to intellectual property and brand protection.
   A: AMD focuses on patents (approximately 13,200 issued patents and 6,600 pending applications worldwide), cross-licensing agreements with other semiconductor firms, and trade secrets to protect its processor designs and architectures. PepsiCo's IP strategy centers on trademarks for its portfolio of global brands (Lay's, Doritos, Pepsi, Gatorade, etc.), long-term trademark licensing arrangements (e.g., Lipton, Starbucks, Dr Pepper), and some patents related to products, packaging, and manufacturing processes. AMD's IP generates licensing revenue, while PepsiCo's brands are the core of its consumer-facing competitive advantage.

8. Q: Describe the seasonal patterns in revenue for AMD and PepsiCo. How do they differ?
   A: AMD's revenue is historically higher in the second half of the year than the first, driven by PC and consumer electronics cycles, though product transitions and market conditions can alter this pattern. PepsiCo's sales are generally highest in the third quarter due to summer seasonal and holiday-related consumption patterns and lowest in the first quarter, though overall seasonality has not had a material impact on consolidated results.

9. Q: Summarize and compare the total amounts each company spent on R&D (AMD) and advertising/marketing (PepsiCo) in 2022. What does this suggest about their business models?
   A: AMD spent $5,005 million on R&D and PepsiCo spent $5,200 million on advertising and other marketing activities (including $3,500 million on advertising alone). This reflects AMD's technology-driven model where innovation in chip design is the primary competitive driver versus PepsiCo's brand-driven model where consumer awareness and marketing are the primary competitive levers. Despite vastly different industries, both allocate similar absolute dollar amounts to their core competitive investment.

10. Q: Aggregate the total operating income/profit across all of AMD's four reportable segments and compare it with the total across PepsiCo's seven divisions (before corporate unallocated) for fiscal year 2022.
   A: AMD's four segments generated combined operating income of $6,243 million (Data Center $1,848M + Client $1,190M + Gaming $953M + Embedded $2,252M), though total consolidated operating income was $1,264M after $4,979M in "All Other" losses. PepsiCo's seven divisions generated $13,615 million in combined operating profit before corporate unallocated expenses of $2,103M, yielding total consolidated operating profit of $11,512M.

11. Q: Compare how AMD and PepsiCo address currency/foreign exchange risk in their 10-K filings.
   A: AMD notes that substantially all product sales are denominated in U.S. dollars, so currency risk primarily affects foreign-denominated operating expenses (payroll, etc.), and the company uses foreign currency forward contracts maturing within 18 months to hedge. PepsiCo faces more direct revenue-side currency exposure since 43% of net revenue is generated outside the U.S.; foreign exchange reduced PepsiCo's 2022 net revenue growth by 3 percentage points, and PepsiCo uses derivative instruments including foreign exchange contracts on both revenue and cost exposure.

12. Q: Compare the human capital and workforce strategies described by AMD and PepsiCo. What similarities and differences exist in how each company discusses its workforce?
   A: Both emphasize attracting and retaining talent as critical, promote diversity, equity and inclusion, and invest in training and development. AMD (approximately 25,000 employees) highlights its technology culture and engineering workforce, CEO Dr. Lisa Su's recognition as a leader, and absorbing approximately 10,000 employees through acquisitions. PepsiCo (approximately 315,000 employees, including 132,000 in the U.S.) emphasizes physical safety (injury-free workplaces, fleet telematics), collective bargaining agreements, its pep+ sustainability strategy engaging employees, and over 1 million hours of training completed in 2022.

13. Q: What were the largest single-customer revenue concentrations disclosed by AMD and PepsiCo, and how do the two companies' customer concentration risks compare?
   A: AMD disclosed one customer (from its Gaming segment) accounting for 16% of consolidated net revenue. PepsiCo disclosed that Walmart and its affiliates (including Sam's Club) accounted for approximately 14% of consolidated net revenue. Both note that the loss of these customers would have a material adverse effect. PepsiCo's concentration is in a retail channel partner, while AMD's is in a gaming console manufacturer.

14. Q: Compare the balance sheet composition of AMD and PepsiCo. What are the largest assets for each company, and what does this reveal about their respective business models?
   A: AMD's largest assets are goodwill ($24,177M) and acquisition-related intangibles ($24,118M), together comprising about 71% of total assets ($67,580M), reflecting the Xilinx acquisition's dominance. PepsiCo's largest assets are PP&E ($24,291M), goodwill ($18,202M), and other indefinite-lived intangible assets ($14,309M), reflecting its capital-intensive manufacturing/distribution infrastructure and brand portfolio. This reveals AMD as an IP/acquisition-driven company with minimal physical assets, versus PepsiCo as a brand-and-factory-driven company with heavy tangible and intangible investments.

15. Q: Describe and compare the sustainability and ESG-related disclosures in AMD's and PepsiCo's 10-K filings. Which company provides more detail?
   A: PepsiCo provides substantially more ESG detail through its pep+ (PepsiCo Positive) strategic framework with three pillars: Positive Agriculture (regenerative practices across 7 million acres), Positive Value Chain (net-zero emissions by 2040, net water positive by 2030, reducing plastic packaging), and Positive Choices (healthier product reformulations). AMD's 10-K has comparatively limited ESG discussion, primarily mentioning environmental law compliance, conflict minerals disclosures, and general statements about diversity and inclusion culture without a comprehensive sustainability strategy framework.

16. Q: Compare the acquisition and divestiture activity described in AMD's and PepsiCo's 2022 10-K filings. What were the major transactions and their strategic rationale?
   A: AMD completed two major acquisitions: Xilinx ($48.8 billion, February 2022) to expand into FPGAs and adaptive SoCs for data center and embedded markets, and Pensando ($1.9 billion, May 2022) to add DPU capabilities for cloud/enterprise customers. PepsiCo's major transaction was a divestiture: selling its Tropicana, Naked, and other juice brands to PAI Partners while retaining a 39% interest in the Tropicana Brands Group joint venture, generating a gain of approximately $3.3 billion. AMD's strategy was inorganic growth through technology portfolio expansion, while PepsiCo's was portfolio optimization by shedding lower-margin juice brands.
